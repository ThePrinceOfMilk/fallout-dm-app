Balance Options

Twenty-one fixes and bundles, each run through the same balance test: every Small Gun and Melee weapon scored over all 100 rolls, and 200 seeded 1-on-1 duels against every enemy at every tier. Other weapon types are left out, as you asked, so enemies here carry only small guns and melee. Every scenario uses the same reference loadout per tier, so the numbers compare like for like.

How to read the numbers

  • Melee ÷ guns: the median melee weapon's damage per turn divided by the median small gun's, at each tier, with no armor on the target. 1 means they're even, and today melee leads.
  • Auto ÷ rifles: how far burst weapons (SMGs, assault rifles) out-damage single-shot rifles. Today it's 1.38.
  • Wins: how often a reference player beats a same-tier enemy 1 on 1, starting 10 m apart. 50% is an even fight. In a party game, around 60-75% per enemy feels fair, because the party gangs up but also gets ganged up on.
  • HP left: how hurt the player is after winning, which is the real cost of a fight between rests.

A real fight is 4 players against a group, and that changes the creature numbers most. A tier 6 creature that wins every 1-on-1 can still fall to four players focusing it, so read creature wins as "how scary this is to face alone".

What the bundles do

Fixing guns versus melee

Today melee does 2.3 · 1.34 · 1.42 · 1.51 times the damage of guns at tiers 1-4. It hits 45% plus skill at any range, while the median gun hits 10% at 10 m at tier 1. There are two ways at it: make guns hit more, or make melee cost more.

1. Raise every gun's hit row by 15%

How: add 0.15 to each value in the Small Guns hit rows (R1-R10).

With this1.46 · 0.81 · 1 · 1.171.4625% / 49%85% · 60% · 50% · 63% · 29%100% · 100% · 100% · 100% · 37% · 1%

What it fixes. Guns and melee become even from tier 2 (ratio about 1), and a pistol at 10 m hits 1 in 4 instead of 1 in 10. Shooting stops being a waste of AP. Fights against tier 2 humans take 3.8 rounds instead of 6.4.

What else it does. Enemies shoot better too, so it helps both sides almost equally: wins against tier 3-5 humans move only a few points. Bursts gain the most, because more hits means more bullets landing, so auto ÷ rifles goes up to 1.46. Tier 1 melee still leads, because tier 1 guns are pistols with low base numbers.

How it feels at the table. Firefights become the main event, and cover and range matter more because more shots connect. It's the most direct fix, but pair it with the burst fix.

Fixing bursts

Fixing creatures

Creature attacks have no Pen, so any armor cuts them to the 20% floor. A player wins about 100% against tier 1-4 creatures, then hits a wall at tier 5-6.

Fixes outside combat math

These weren't simulated, because they're about data or pacing rather than the dice.

Ammo cleanup

Fix the misfiled "5.56mm - API" (should be 5.56mm NATO). Add a Standard load to calibers that lack one, and fill in the blank loads (2mm EC, .44 Mysterious, two 20 Gauge loads).

What it fixes. Every gun gets a sensible default load, and the vendor and enemy generator stop falling back to odd loads.

What else it does. None, beyond the time it takes.

Dead ammo loads

42 loads are worse than another load of the same caliber at the same or lower tier. Either make them cheaper (once ammo has prices), or give each a niche: a status effect, more rounds per box, or a lower tier.

What it fixes. Ammo choice becomes a real decision instead of always picking the same load.

What else it does. More rows to keep balanced.

Prices for ammo and armor

No ammo or armor has a value, so the vendor uses placeholders (1 cap × tier per round, flat prices for armor).

What it fixes. Barter, looting and vendor tiers start to mean something.

What else it does. Needs a pass over about 290 rows. A formula would do: say, ammo = (damage + Pen) / 4 caps per round, and armor = DT² caps.

Helmets at tiers 2, 3 and 5

There are none, so players keep a tier 1 helmet through tier 3.

What it fixes. Head armor keeps pace with the tier.

What else it does. Raises player DT a little, making those tiers slightly easier.

Armor for enemy loadouts

Most tier 1-3 human enemies use the workbook's fixed loadouts, which list no armor (2 of 55 do), then armor jumps at tier 4.

What it fixes. Smooths the armor jump at tier 4, and makes AP ammo worth something early.

What else it does. Low tiers get a bit harder.

Robots that shoot

Sentry Bots, Eyebots, Mister Gutsys, Protectrons and Securitrons only have melee attacks. Give them ranged attacks with hit rows (they can use Small Guns-style rows).

What it fixes. Robots play like robots, and fights with them use range and cover.

What else it does. Needs new attack rows. Turrets and robots become harder.

Tier 6 humans

There are none, so tier 6 is creatures only.

What it fixes. A full ladder for human factions.

What else it does. New enemy rows.

XP pacing

Each level needs 100 × level XP, and a fight with 4 tier 1 enemies gives each player 20 XP. That's about 75 fights to leave tier 1 and around 440 to reach level 30. Options: double tier 1-2 XP, or make each level need 50 × level, which halves everything (about 220 fights).

What it fixes. Early levels come at a steadier pace.

What else it does. Faster levelling means tiers go by faster, so loot and vendors need to keep up.

Missing enemy data

Assaultron has no HP or XP, and its "Assaultron Arm" attack doesn't exist. Powder Gangers, Sunny Smiles and Displaced Resident give no XP.

What it fixes. Those enemies work properly.

What else it does. None.

What I'd do

Start with Balanced B, then adjust creature Pen between 2 and 4 per tier to taste. It's the only option that keeps every tier from 1 to 5 in a fair band without making any weapon style the obvious pick. Then do the data fixes in any order: ammo cleanup and enemy armor first, since they affect every fight.

If you'd rather change as little as possible, Light touch gets most of the way with small numbers. If guns should be the heart of the game, take Balanced A and give tier 3 guns a small bump.

Re-run any scenario from the app folder, for example: BAL_TABS=SG,MW BAL_SKILLDIV=100 BAL_PCHP=3 node balance.js out.json. Switches: BAL_HITSHIFT, BAL_SKILLDIV, BAL_MELEEBASE, BAL_MELEEAP, BAL_CRITALL, BAL_BURSTAP, BAL_CREATUREPEN, BAL_FLOOR, BAL_PCHP, BAL_PCAP, BAL_NPCHP, BAL_NPCDT, BAL_MOBHP.
