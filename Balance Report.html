Balance Test

Weapons, armor, ammo and enemies, run through the app's own combat math: hit chance, the 00-99 roll bands, bursts, crits, jams, reloads and DT. Workbook data from 2026-11-09. Every attack is scored over all 100 rolls, and the 1-on-1 duels are 200 seeded fights per enemy.

What the test found

  1. Critical

    Half the weapons can't be used yet

    282 of 572 weapons have no hit chance or damage. Big Guns stop after tier 1 (12 of 111 usable). Energy Weapons stop after tier 2 (22 of 90). Melee has 6 of 20 at tier 5 and none at tier 6. Explosives: 12 of 74 have numbers. Only Small Guns are complete (151 of 158).

    Past tier 2, a Big Guns or Energy build has nothing to buy. Enemies with those skills fall back to tier 1 or 2 weapons.

  2. Critical

    Melee out-damages guns through tier 4

    The median melee weapon does 2.3x the damage per turn of the median small gun at tier 1, then 1.3x, 1.4x and 1.5x at tiers 2-4. Melee always hits at 45% plus skill, at any range, for 2-3 AP. Guns hit 28% at their best range and 18% at 10 m. Walking 10 m only costs a melee fighter 4 AP. Guns pull ahead at tier 5, where only 6 melee weapons have numbers.

    Your current party shows it: for everyone who carries a gun, bare-fist Basic Fighting does more. Pipe Shotgun 0 at 10 m vs fists 17.1; Pipe Flamer 0 at 10 m vs fists 14.1; Pipe Pistol 2.7 at 10 m vs fists 16.5 (damage per turn).

  3. Serious

    Hit chances are low, and skill barely raises them

    Hit chance is the weapon's range value plus (skill - requirement) / 200, so 10 skill points add only 5%. With the skill a tier usually asks for, the median gun hits 28% at its best range and 18% at 10 m: about three shots in four miss. At skill 100 the median weapon reaches 60%, and only 19 of 290 ever reach the 95% cap.

  4. Serious

    Burst guns dominate, and SMGs break the tier ladder

    A crit or super crit lands every bullet in a burst, and a high roll lands more of them. Auto weapons do a median 22.3 per turn, against 13.8 for rifles, 16.2 for snipers and 9.9 for pistols. Tier 2 SMGs (Classic 9mm SMG 26.4, 10mm SMG 25.6) beat the tier 3 median of 12.3. 38 weapons beat the next tier's median.

  5. Serious

    Creature attacks have no Pen, so any armor cuts them to 20%

    All 78 creature attacks are melee, with no Pen and no range. Against any DT, an attack with no Pen falls to the 20% damage floor. Sentry Bots, Eyebots, Mister Gutsys and Protectrons punch instead of shooting. A tier-matched player wins 98%-100% of duels against tier 1-4 creatures, then 3% at tier 5 and 0% at tier 6. The cliff comes from raw size: tier 6 creatures have a median 135 HP, against the player's 54.

  6. Serious

    Tier 5 is a wall

    Against same-tier humans, a player wins 88% at T1, 42% at T2, 47% at T3, 52% at T4, 22% at T5. At tier 5, enemy DT (median 24.8) outpaces the ammo most guns can load. The player needs about 11.3 turns to drop one, and goes down in about 6. There are no tier 6 humans at all.

  7. Warning

    Armor jumps at tier 4, and most guns can't keep up

    DT works as a Pen check: Pen at or above DT does full damage, and below it damage is cut to Pen / DT, with a 20% floor. Standard ammo Pen trails the median player kit every tier: 6 vs 8, 8 vs 12, 16 vs 19, 28 vs 29 at tiers 2-5. Most enemy humans at tiers 1-3 wear nothing, because the workbook's fixed loadouts list no armor. Then enemy DT jumps to 16 at tier 4, which only 7 of 28 tier 4 guns can pierce with any load.

  8. Warning

    Ammo has dead loads, missing prices and a typo

    42 loads are worse than another load of the same caliber at the same or lower tier. 8 loads beat Standard outright at no extra tier, mostly Hand Loads. 12 calibers have no Standard load. 6 loads have no numbers, including both 2mm EC loads. The Special caliber (Dart Gun, General Liu Rifle, Syringer) has no ammo. "5.56mm - API" is filed under 5.56mm, not 5.56mm NATO, so no gun can load it. No ammo or armor has a price.

  9. Warning

    Data gaps in the enemy list

    Powder Ganger 1 (no XP); Powder Ganger 2 (no XP); Powder Ganger Ringleader (no XP); Powder Ganger Sharpshooter (no XP); Sunny Smiles (no XP); Displaced Resident (no XP); Assaultron (no HP, no XP, Assaultron Arm doesn't exist).

How the test works

A reference player stands in for a character at each tier: level 5 x tier - 2, SPECIAL S6 P6 E6 C5 I6 A5 L6, and the skill the tier's typical weapon asks for (the same rule the enemy generator uses). They wear the tier's median body armor and helmet, so armor that lowers Agility costs them AP, as it would at the table. The app's current settings are used as they are: no Perception or Strength penalty, a 95% cap and a 20% damage floor.

Damage per turn is the average over all 100 rolls, times the attacks that fit in the turn's AP. AP lost to jams and reloads is spread over the magazine. Shots are at the Body. Duels start 10 m apart, with rolled initiative. Melee fighters walk in, and each side uses whichever weapon does most that turn. The reference player carries the tier's median small gun with its best tier-legal ammo, plus the median melee weapon.

Ammo

Standard ammo never quite pierces same-tier armor. Better loads close some of the gap: with the best load a tier allows, the median small gun goes from 5, 8.8, 12.3, 7.9, 10.8, 29.8 to 6.3, 11.3, 18, 14.8, 19.1, 57.2 damage per turn against typical enemy armor. That makes ammo choice matter, which is good. But it also means Standard is rarely the right pick past tier 2.

"Stops" means the load's Pen is below the kit's DT, so its damage is cut. From tier 2 up, a median kit cuts 73-84% of same-tier loads and lets 52%-66% of their damage through. That's strong, but not absolute: a load one tier up still gets 61%-82% through.

  • There are no helmets at tiers 2, 3 and 5, so the reference kit keeps a tier 1 helmet through tier 3.
  • Armor that lowers Agility costs AP. The median tier 4 kit leaves the player at 9 AP, and tier 6 at 8.
  • Enemies wear armor unevenly: 35% at T1, 34% at T2, 44% at T3, 57% at T4, 100% at T5. Most tier 1-3 humans use the workbook's fixed loadouts, which list no armor (only 2 of 55 do).
  • No body armor or helmet has a price.

What I'd change, in order

1. Fill in the missing weaponsbiggest gap

Big Guns from tier 2, Energy Weapons from tier 3, Melee at tiers 5-6, and Explosives. Until then those skills are dead ends, and the vendor and enemy generator can only offer tier 1-2 versions of them.

2. Close the melee-gun gappick one

Either raise every gun's hit row by about +0.15, or double how much skill counts, from (skill - req) / 200 to / 100. The first fixes the typical case; the second rewards investing in a skill. Lowering melee to 35% helps a little, but not enough alone. Retest tier 4-5 humans afterwards, because they benefit too.

3. Give creature attacks Penone column

About 4 per tier (Deathclaw ~20) makes mid-tier creatures dangerous without changing their damage. While you're there, give robots ranged attacks with hit rows: Sentry Bot, Eyebots, Mister Gutsy, Protectron, Securitron.

4. Smooth tier 5the wall

Tier 5 enemies have DT ~25 and tier 5-6 creatures have HP 50-300, while the player has ~50 HP. Options: +2 HP per level for players instead of +1, cap generated enemy armor at the tier's typical ammo Pen, or add tier 5-6 ammo with Pen 30+. Also add tier 6 humans.

5. Rein in burstsSMGs

Either land 1 bullet per crit instead of all of them (the critAllBullets setting), or raise burst AP costs. Check The Jam, Classic 9mm SMG, 10mm SMG, Handmade Rifle and Veruc after.

6. Clean up ammo and priceshousekeeping

Fix "5.56mm" to "5.56mm NATO". Add Standard loads to the 12 calibers without one. Finish the 6 blank loads and give the Special caliber an ammo row. Either make the 42 dead loads cheaper, or give them a niche. Add prices for ammo and armor. Add helmets at tiers 2, 3 and 5. Fill in the enemy data gaps (Assaultron, the Powder Gangers' XP).
